# P7-03 federation envelopes, descriptors, and replay

## The rule

**Resolving an identity never grants authority.** Any authenticated workload in the
federation may resolve another's minimal descriptor. Every action is still authorized
*at the destination, by the destination, against its own records* — whatever the source
believed when it sent the envelope.

Everything below exists to make that rule survive a hostile or merely broken peer.

## What the destination checks, before authorization

The version it speaks; that the envelope is addressed to **it**, not merely handed to
it; that source and destination differ; that it has not expired by the destination's own
clock; that its lifetime is at most 60 seconds; that its hop limit is at most 4; that
its nonce is present, bounded, and unseen; that the payload digest is a real SHA-256
digest; and that it names a subject, a target, and an action.

Only then is the subject reauthorized and the action decided.

## Why those limits

**60 seconds.** The destination must remember every nonce until the envelope expires. A
long-lived envelope is both a replay waiting to happen and an unbounded memory cost.

**Four hops.** Forwarding decreases the limit and refuses at zero, so a routing loop
ends after at most four hops even if a node misbehaves.

**A repeated nonce is a replay, not a retry.** A retry carries the same `request_id` and
is answered from the record; a repeated nonce means someone is replaying a captured
envelope.

## Descriptors that cannot be rolled back

A cached node descriptor is replaced only by one with a strictly **higher** sequence, so
a replayed older descriptor cannot un-rotate a key or un-revoke an epoch. A descriptor
that names its own key epoch among its revoked epochs is refused outright: a node cannot
publish keys it has itself revoked.

The workload descriptor is deliberately small — workload ID, home node and address,
public key, revision, expiry. It says where to send something and how to verify the
answer, and nothing about the creature, the program, its capabilities, its logs, its
presence, or its data.

## Verification

Thirteen cases in `aseman-domain::federation`, each one a way a peer could be wrong:
an envelope addressed elsewhere, a replayed nonce, an over-long lifetime, an expired
envelope, a hop limit above the maximum and a loop that ends, four malformed digests, a
blank subject, target, or action, a self-addressed envelope, an unknown version, a
replayed older descriptor, a self-revoked epoch, and descriptor revisions with expiry.

## Transport delivered after this work unit

`aseman-federation-http` now owns the real inbound and outbound transport: mandatory
mutual TLS, A401 proof verification bound to the envelope and payload, bounded request
bodies and graceful drain, destination authorization and durable replay, signed-response
verification, bounded exponential retries, and a circuit breaker. An executor failure
is not persisted as a successful answer, so the request can safely recover.

## Node composition and remaining observation

The node now composes both directions. Outbound canonical workload actions resolve the
home-node and peer descriptors, repeat source authorization, sign an A401 proof with
the current node epoch, send with mutual TLS, and accept an answer only after a current
descriptor key verifies it. Descriptor sequence changes replace the cached endpoint,
key verifier, and per-peer circuit on the next request. Inbound composition verifies
the proof and target/payload binding before the shared action executor runs.

What remains is operational evidence: two deployed clusters must observe a live key
rotation and partition recovery. The package-level live test proves durable two-cluster
semantics but is not deployment evidence.
